The lecture set out four centuries of finders' law and none of it
settles what this Office does. This shift writes that answer down.

## Before the shift

- Read the four cases from the lecture again: *Armory*, *Bridges*,
  *Hannah v Peel* and *Parker*.
- Bring one sentence stating what you think the Office should be allowed
  to do with something nobody claims.

## At the counter

- **0–15 min.** In groups, draft a clause covering who the Office
  considers the finder, the occupier and the claimant to be.
- **15–35 min.** Test your draft against three scenarios: an object found
  in the tutorial room, an object found by a tutor doing their job, and an
  object found by a cleaner doing theirs. Note anywhere the draft gives a
  different answer than the class expects.
- **35–45 min.** Combine every group's clauses into one notice, fixing
  any place two clauses disagree.

## What leaves with you

The Office's terms and conditions notice, tested against three
scenarios it has to be able to answer.

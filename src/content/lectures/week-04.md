A phone, locked, battery dead, is the strangest object the Office handles
because it might already know exactly where it is — it just cannot say so
to the person holding it, and would not necessarily be allowed to even if
it could. Last week's intake form assumed the Office has to write a
description down. This week's question: what happens when the object
knows where it is?

## A lock screen is a description that refuses to speak

Every other object on the shelf is described from the outside: colour,
size, a scratch. A phone carries its own record of who it belongs to,
behind a lock screen built specifically so a stranger who finds it cannot
read that record. The Office cannot get past the lock any more than a
finder can, so a phone sits on the shelf exactly as unidentified as a set
of keys — despite holding, somewhere inside it, the one fact that would
close the case immediately.

## Finding versus following

Phones, tags and trackers can also report a location without anyone
touching the device — which is exactly what an owner wants from a lost
phone, and exactly what a stranger being tracked without consent does not
want done to their bag. Apple and Google's joint specification for
detecting unwanted location trackers exists because the same feature —
report this object's location to someone else — serves both cases from
identical hardware, and the difference between them is entirely who is
asking and whether the object's owner knows.

The Office's policy question follows directly: if a deposit could be
tagged and tracked, who is allowed to see where it has been, and does the
depositor get a say before it happens?

## Reading

The Apple/Google industry specification for detecting unwanted location
trackers (IETF draft, 2023–24). Read the problem statement for why
"locate it" and "track it" turned out to need the same answer for two very
different audiences.

## What the Office gains

A tracking policy, voted on by the class, stating whether deposits may be
tagged at all and, if so, who is told where they have been.

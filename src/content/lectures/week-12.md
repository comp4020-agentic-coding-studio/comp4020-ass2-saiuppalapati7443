The box on the table this week holds this semester's own ledgers, tags
and tickets — everything the Office has produced about everyone else's
objects. Week 11 asked what happens when the lost thing is looking for
you too; this week turns that question back on the Office itself: would
your Office give you your own thing back?

## A system's behaviour comes from its structure

Meadows argues that a system does not behave the way it does because of
the people operating it in any given moment; it behaves the way its
structure makes it behave, and changing an outcome means changing the
structure, not exhorting the people inside it to try harder. The Office's
return rate this semester was not decided by how conscientious any one
student was at the counter. It was decided, piece by piece, by the
eleven structures the class chose to build — starting the very first week
this course ran.

| 12 | The box      | The system     |

Each row left the Office holding something it did not have the week
before: a deposit, a hand-in point, an intake form, a tracking policy,
shelving, a claim protocol, triage rules, terms and conditions, an audit,
a disposal policy, a matching exercise with another Office. None of those
eleven pieces was optional; a weak one anywhere in the row is the reason
an object fails to come home this week, and the class will be able to
point to which row it was.

## Reclaim Day is the test, not a ceremony

Today each student retrieves the object they deposited in week 1 —
through the Office's own procedure, using whichever intake form, claim
protocol and shelving scheme the class actually built, not by asking Otto
to hand it back directly. The class's return rate is reported, not
graded, because grading it would reward the wrong thing: a system built
to pass an assessment is not the same system as one built to actually
return people's things, and the whole semester has been an argument for
why that distinction matters.

## Reading

Meadows (2008), *Thinking in Systems*. Read it for the claim that
structure, not effort, determines outcome — then decide whether your own
Office's return rate proves or disproves it.

## What the Office gains

Reclaim Day: the semester's one direct test of the one idea this course
opened with — that things are not lost, only unrecorded, and whether one
comes home is a property of the system that caught it.

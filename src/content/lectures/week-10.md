A bicycle with two frame numbers, one of them filed to match a stolen
register, is the object this course finally has to let go of. Week 9
asked why some deposits are never reclaimed; this week asks what happens
once the Office accepts that one of them is not going to be: when does
lost become gone?

## A retention period is a decision, not a fact

No object is held forever. At some point a jurisdiction's rules — or, in
the absence of any that apply, the Office's own terms and conditions from
week 8 — say how long an unclaimed deposit sits on the shelf before it
can be auctioned, donated or destroyed. That period is not a natural
fact about the object; it is a choice about how long "someone might still
come back" outweighs "this shelf space could hold something else."

## The frame number is week 3's problem, come back around

A bicycle's frame number exists so the object can be identified even
after everything about its appearance has changed — the same job the
intake form from week 3 does for a set of keys. A filed and re-stamped
number is an attempt to defeat exactly that: to make an object that
should be traceable back to an owner or a theft report untraceable
instead. Disposal decisions built on "nobody has claimed this" only work
if the description the Office is disposing of was trustworthy in the
first place.

## Choose the destination, do not default into it

Auction returns money, usually to the Office or its institution rather
than the original owner. Donation returns use, to someone other than the
original owner. Destruction returns nothing to anyone. None of the three
is obviously correct for a bicycle with a tampered frame number; a
disposal policy has to say, in advance, which of the three applies to
which kind of unclaimed object and why, rather than leaving the choice to
whoever is closing out the shelf that week.

## Reading

No source is vetted for retention periods or disposal rules generally —
they are set by whatever jurisdiction and institution the Office actually
sits in. Find your own jurisdiction's rules on unclaimed property before
the shift and bring the citation with you; do not treat any rule stated
here as fact.

## What the Office gains

A disposal policy: which unclaimed deposits are auctioned, donated or
destroyed, after how long, and who decides — tested this week against a
bicycle whose own identity has been deliberately made hard to trust.

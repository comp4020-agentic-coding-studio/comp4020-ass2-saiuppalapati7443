## The brief

> Design a form a stranger could use to log an object at intake, then prove
> it works on someone who is not you.

Week 3 argued that free text fails as a description and that any fixed set
of fields will lose something. This assignment asks you to feel that
trade-off yourself: build a form, then hand it to a stranger with ten
objects they have never seen before and watch where it fails them.

## What you submit

- The first version of your intake form.
- A record of the test: what the stranger was given, what they wrote on the
  form, and where the form and the objects disagreed.
- A second version of the form, changed once in response to what the test
  showed, with a short note on what the change cost you.

## How it connects

This builds directly on the keys problem from week 3: a scheme built around
one kind of feature loses whatever it did not think to ask about. By the
time this is due, the Office has been logging real deposits against a form
for months, and the audit that opens this week asks what became of
everything a form like yours failed to describe well enough to find again.
